About Me

After a seven-month US Army deployment training, I’m actively looking to land my first job/internship in software engineering. I will integrate what I’ve learned from both the military and university to achieve results both for myself and for the organization that I work for. From NJIT, I’ve gained a solid foundation in foundations of computer science, programing languages' concepts, and have great problem-solving skills. From the US Army, I’ve gained the following skills: leadership, self-sufficiency, effective communication, teamwork, Ability to Perform Under Pressure, as well as the Seven Army Values. Also, I’m currently enrolled in a course to acquire certain IT certificates, starting with Security+ but not limited to PennTest.

Background

I came from a developing country where I had virtually no access to computers. Coming here to America and having access to computers allowed me to see the world in the information age that it’s in. Many technologies taking for granted here in the U.S. is Godsent in other parts of the world. So, my goal is to eventually help decrease the IT gap between developed countries and my own. I will start by creating tutorial sessions or write some books in my native language for those willing and able to learn the basics.

Eager to Learn

The first time I used a computer, I was curious to know how they functioned, the philosophy behind computers, and the methods of how those philosophies came about. Studying Computers Science at NJIT (New Jersey Institute of Technology) has only left me even hungrier to know more. It would appear the more I know, the more I want to know.

Think Fast

My MOS in the Army is a 25U Signal Support Specialist. A 25U must have the wherewithal to break down any problem and come up with a system/solution. My experience with the Army showed me how to think on my feet and come up with solutions for unforeseen problems on the spot. Uncertainty in the army is common and can be stressful. However, that stress did not get the better of me, because I overcame it by thinking on your feet. This helped me to stay cool and confident when under pressure, and to deliver assured and confident answers even when faced with unexpected questions.
